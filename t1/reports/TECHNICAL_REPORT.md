# Do language models have an internal algebra of physical units? A categorical dimension code, causally read as a bound number–unit representation

*Technical report, attempt t1 (2026-09-13). Every claim below is backed by `CLAIMS.md` at its recorded strength; experiment IDs refer to `NOTEBOOK.md`. Figures regenerate with `make figures`.*

## Abstract
A quantity mention like "5 meters" carries a *dimension* — a point L¹M⁰T⁰ on the free abelian group of exponent vectors — and physics licenses addition, comparison and conversion only between quantities at the same point. Prior mechanistic work settled the neighbouring latent variables (numeric value: helical; scale: heuristic; binding: additive IDs) but left dimension uncharacterised. We ask whether pretrained LMs maintain a dimension variable with the group's geometry and use it to license operations. Behaviourally, six base models (Qwen3-4B/8B/14B, OLMo-3-7B/32B, Gemma-2-9B) select the dimensionally correct composed unit for invented lexemes and unnamed lattice points at 0.84–0.97 (4-way), with no surface-order penalty — but 40–85% of that accuracy is available from the relation wording alone, and free generation drops exponents. Representationally, the dimension of a mentioned quantity is linearly decodable across held-out unit lexemes and is carried to the referent in all five probed models (categorical probes 0.64–0.74 on 12 lattice points, control ≈ 0.07), yet it has **no lattice geometry**: held-out named lattice points do not extrapolate (0.03–0.09), centroids are not additive, and dimensionless quantities are not at the origin. The representation is categorical — one direction per dimension — not a coordinate code. Causally, quantity 1's unit representation is read out at early layers of the unit token; a learned rank-64 subspace there reaches balanced interchange accuracy 0.60 under a dimension-equality model and 0.62 under the model's own pairwise-compatibility table (random 0.50; ceilings 0.65 / 0.78), generalises to unseen lexemes, is necessary and dose-monotone — but where the two models disagree its effects follow the pairwise table (decisively so in Gemma-2-9B: 0.68 vs 0.44), it is bound with the numeral (value readout R² 0.96 → 0.55 after a swap), and it does nothing for invented units. We conclude that current LMs have a lexeme-general categorical dimension code but no algebra: licensing is computed by a pairwise heuristic over bound number–unit representations, mirroring the heuristic account previously established for scale. All negative results carry the same control regime as the positive ones.

## 1. Introduction
Units of measurement are the everyday face of a type system. Kennedy's units-of-measure types show that dimensional consistency is exactly abelian-group arithmetic on exponent vectors; a model that "understood" units in this sense would carry a variable ranging over that lattice and gate operations by equality on it. Recent interpretability results make the question sharp: numeric value has a causally verified helical geometry used by a "Clock" addition algorithm; scale (unit magnitude) was shown in June 2026 to be handled by separate numeral and unit-scale heuristics rather than by conversion to a shared scale; binding of values to referents uses additive binding-ID vectors. Dimension — the variable that makes "5 m + 3 s" ill-typed — had not been tested as a representation, as a geometry, or as a causally used variable. We test all three, against a ladder of hypotheses (H1 decodability, H2 lattice geometry, H3 causal gating, H4 productive composition) and a set of rival accounts (lexical co-occurrence, template retrieval, bag of heuristics, graded algebra, the algebra), with pre-registered predictions and decision rules at three gates.

## 2. Related work
Sasaki et al. (2026) — quantity comparison as numeral- and unit-scale heuristics, established by surrogate regression and DAS interchange; our methodological template and our null. Kantamneni & Tegmark (2025) — parametric fit → patching of the fit as the standard for a causally verified structured representation; we apply the same logic to exponent-vector fits. Nanda, Lee & Wattenberg (2023) — the parameterisation lesson (Othello), which we operationalise by comparing linear, sign/magnitude, categorical and MLP parameterisations before drawing verdicts. Feng & Steinhardt (2024) — binding IDs; source of our binding-contamination controls and the observation that the causally effective unit representation is bound with the numeral. Park et al. (2025) — structure-to-geometry papers with shuffled-label and random-baseline controls, which we mirror for the group structure. Methods: Zhang & Nanda; Heimersheim & Nanda; Geiger et al. (DAS, causal abstraction); Makelov, Lange & Nanda (subspace-patching illusion; we adopt natural-projection, random-subspace, probe-derived and full-patching comparisons). Nearest neighbour: TypeProbe (types in code models; no causal step, no lattice).

## 3. Methods
**Stimuli.** One Pint-validated library (`uot`) generates every task from a unit registry (SI/prefixed, imperial, archaic, nautical, astronomical, 80+ named derived units; es/de/fr forms), an exponent-vector algebra, invented lexemes (CVC nonsense words verified to have ≤ 50 Dolma-1.7 occurrences via infini-gram; every composed answer string ≤ 1), and invented base dimensions. Tasks: T1 unit-of-answer cloze (named relations, unnamed lattice points such as kg²·m and L³/T⁵, invented lexemes, invented base dimensions, each with familiar twins; ≥ 2 templates per relation in both surface orders; five surface styles; length-matched, prefix-free candidate sets), T2 consistency, T3 formula selection, T4 conversion acceptability, T5 error spotting; definition-by-example (arithmetic-only) and integer-output variants; Phase-1 mention passages with an anaphoric continuation (base, named, compound-lattice, dimensionless, invented, cross-lingual; neutral / revealing / noun-only noun families); Phase-2 two-quantity judgments over 43 single-token lexemes at 10 lattice points; 2,370 naturalistic mentions mined from Wikipedia.
**Models.** Qwen3-4B/8B/14B-Base, OLMo-3-7B/32B, Gemma-2-9B; Qwen3-4B/8B instruct (thinking off). bf16 on A6000/A100.
**Readouts.** Candidate readout = argmax of mean per-token log-prob over K candidates (pre-registered primary); bias-free readouts for yes/no and letter tasks (AUROC of the margin; median-calibrated accuracy; per-position calibration); dimension-level generation readout via a parser that maps free-form unit expressions (including invented lexemes recovered from context) to a dimension.
**Probes.** PCA-512 → ridge (LOO-CV α) to the (L, M, T) exponent vector; multinomial logistic over lattice points; sign/magnitude; MLP. Splits: random, held-out expressions, leave-one-lexeme-out with the point retained, held-out lattice points (base points kept). Control task with consistent random lexeme→point assignment. Metrics: nearest-lattice-point, per-axis, sign, R².
**Interventions.** Own implementation of distributed interchange (orthogonal learned subspaces; matched-rank random; probe-derived; full residual) with forward hooks at the residual stream of chosen tokens/layers; balanced interchange-intervention accuracy relative to the model's own base prediction (chance 0.5). Rival causal models fully specified: M_alg (categorical D1; yes iff D1 = D2) and M_heur (lexeme U1; the model's own 52×52 pairwise table, rank-calibrated). Controls: matched-rank random (seeds), probe-derived subspaces, rank sweep, full-patch ceiling, natural-distribution projection, noising, dose–response, value preservation, binding, text-rewrite reference.
**Pre-registration.** `PREDICTIONS_phase{0,1,2,3}.md` committed before each phase's headline runs; decision records `DR-001…005`; every anomaly logged the day found (`NOTEBOOK.md`).

## 4. Results
### 4.1 Behaviour (Phase 0; C1–C6)
All six base models compose units for invented lexemes (0.89–0.97), invented base dimensions (0.88–0.94) and unnamed lattice points (0.84–0.91) far above the 0.25 chance, with order-swapped items no harder than in-order ones (Fig. 1–2). But deleting the unit tokens from the scenario while keeping the relation sentence (and, for invented units, the definitions) retains 0.38–0.86 (Fig. 3): a verbal route explains much of the competence; the full prompt adds +0.08 to +0.47. With the relation given only as arithmetic (never named), accuracy is 0.69–0.86, and dropping the lexeme definitions altogether costs only 0.05–0.10 — composition is largely symbolic exponent bookkeeping over whatever lexemes appear. Free generation is dimensionally correct in 42–83% of generations containing a unit (dropped exponents dominate the errors); with integer outputs it rises to 0.71–0.77 (named) but stays 0.30–0.69 on unnamed points. Judgment tasks show real ranking signal masked by constant yes/no biases (T2 AUROC 0.82–0.97 for invented units, 0.56–0.95 for familiar pairs; Gemma answers "yes" 75–100% of the time). No behavioural gradient with lattice distance (≤ 8) or corpus frequency exists at this ceiling. Instruct tuning removes the bias but does not improve composition.
### 4.2 Geometry (Phase 1; C7–C10)
Base dimensions decode across unseen lexemes at 0.83–0.93 (unit token), 0.72–0.91 (mention end), 0.67–0.84 (anaphor). On 12 base+named points with 68 lexemes, leave-one-lexeme-out categorical probes reach 0.64–0.74 at the referent in all five models (linear 0.40–0.46; control 0.04–0.09; Fig. G1a), and a base-dimension probe trained on mentions classifies the input-unit tokens of composition prompts at 0.89–0.99. Yet held-out named lattice points do not extrapolate under any parameterisation (nearest 0.03–0.09; sign accuracy 0.61–0.71; Fig. G1b), centroid additivity equals the random-triple baseline (1.22–1.35 vs 1.26–1.36), dimensionless mentions are never at the origin, and the composed answer dimension at the pre-answer token is relation-specific (within-relation 0.78–0.80, across relations 0.04–0.14). Probes on compound expressions ("kg²·m/s²") read the exponents from the surface form and do not transfer to named units — a stimulus lesson we record rather than hide. Gemma Scope latents at the unit token are near-exclusive per lattice point (descriptive).
### 4.3 Causal adjudication (Phase 2; C11–C16)
Full-residual patching of quantity 1's unit token (Fig. P2a, dotted) changes the judgment only at layers 4–12; at layer 4 it follows the model's pairwise table in 73% of changing cases and dimension equality in 40%. A learned rank-64 subspace at layer 8 reaches balanced IIA 0.595 ± 0.003 (M_alg labels) / 0.621 ± 0.027 (M_heur) vs 0.499 ± 0.003 for matched-rank random subspaces (4 seeds); the effect grows monotonically with rank (chance at ranks 1–4; 0.63/0.70 at rank 128) and probe-derived categorical directions recover ≈ 90% of the rank-16 effect while the 3-dimensional exponent basis does nothing. The subspace generalises to lexemes unseen in training (0.61/0.63), separates the natural conditions (0.69 vs 0.55 for a random projection), is necessary (noising flips 49% vs 2%), dose-monotone, and leaves quantity 2's dimension readout intact (0.99). Against the algebra: on the examples where equality and the pairwise table disagree, effects follow the table (0.44 vs 0.52); the equality-trained subspace fits the table's labels better than equality's (0.66 vs 0.60); invented units show zero effect at the lexeme token (their dimension lives in the definition sentence); and the swap is not value-free — the downstream numeral readout of quantity 1 drops from R² 0.96 to 0.55 (random subspace: 0.93). No base-vs-derived gradient (0.57–0.58 vs 0.60). OLMo-3-7B replicates the pattern at layer 4 (alg 0.57, heur 0.58, ceilings 0.62/0.72, disagree 0.44 vs 0.56); Gemma-2-9B, whose raw argmax is a constant "yes", is adjudicated with a bias-corrected readout (threshold at the clean median margin; base accuracy 0.60): ceilings 0.60 / 0.69, learned rank-64 subspaces 0.52 (equality; random 0.49) vs 0.67 (pairwise table, ≈ its ceiling), disagree 0.44 vs 0.68, held-out lexemes 0.53 / 0.64, invented 0.50 — the pre-registered heuristics-extension criterion is met in full for this model. At the output level, implanting slot 2's dimension into slot 1 of a composition prompt suppresses the composed answer as strongly as editing the text (−1.7 vs −1.9 nats) but no lawfully composed alternative was available in the candidate set (exploratory).
### 4.4 Generality and utility (Phase 3)
**Naturalistic transfer (C17).** A categorical probe trained on synthetic mentions classifies 2,322 Wikipedia quantity mentions (12 points, 69 lexemes) at 0.89–0.92 at the unit token without retraining (majority 0.13); within-natural leave-one-lexeme-out 0.43–0.54 vs control ≈ 0.07 (Fig. P3). Referent-position claims were not testable in mined text.
**Utility demo (C18, weak).** On base-model step-by-step solutions with the final unit swapped for a wrong-dimension unit, a probe read-out of the stated unit's dimension detects the swap at AUROC 0.84 (familiar) / 0.99 (invented) versus the model's self-check at 0.70 / 0.74; but extraction of the model's own final answer from free chain-of-thought was unreliable (5–16% dimension-correct), so this is not a utility claim — it needs instruct models with a fixed answer format.
**Robustness.** Template spread, base-vs-instruct and surface-style breakdowns from Phase 0 are in `runs/E0.3/analysis/`; Phase-1 conclusions hold across two stimulus seeds and five models; Phase-2 conclusions across four DAS seeds (Qwen3-4B) and one replicate (OLMo-7B). Gemma-2-9B bias-corrected Phase-2 cells are in §4.3.

## 5. Limitations
Single-site interventions at the lexeme token cannot reach the dimension of invented units (carried by their definition); a definition-token / referent-site DAS is the first item in NEXT.md. The equality model is bounded by the models' own imperfect judgments (ceiling 0.65–0.67), so "no algebra" is partly "no algebra *in the behaviour*"; ceiling-normalised numbers are reported. The named-lattice extrapolation test has only 12 points. Phase 2 has the full control battery for one model (4 seeds), a replicate for a second, and none for the third. Corpus frequency is measured on Dolma 1.7, a proxy for OLMo-3's corpus. Free-generation readouts are coverage-limited.

## 6. Threats to validity
*Construct:* the candidate readout inflates behavioural competence (free generation is much lower) — both are reported. *Internal:* stimulus artefacts were found and fixed during the project (real words among "invented" lexemes; prefix candidates; surface-form exponents in compound-lattice stimuli; multi-token units under single-token patching; a layer-index mismatch in the controls) — each is logged with the pre-fix numbers retained. *Statistical:* bootstrap CIs over templates and items; 2 stimulus seeds (Phases 0–1), 4 DAS seeds; effect sizes are small in absolute terms (IIA 0.60–0.62 vs 0.50) and interpreted relative to full-patch ceilings. *External:* three families, 4B–32B; naturalistic transfer tested on Wikipedia mentions only.

## 7. Conclusion
Language models represent the dimension of a mentioned quantity as a lexeme-general category attached to the referent, but not as a point in an exponent lattice, and they license unit operations through a pairwise-compatibility heuristic acting on bound number–unit representations rather than through equality on a dimension variable. Scale was heuristic; dimension is categorical, and its use is heuristic too.
