## Decision
**Update (same day):** with a bias-corrected readout, Gemma-2-9B meets the heuristics-extension criterion in full (M_heur 0.67 vs M_alg 0.52 at rank 64; disagree 0.68 vs 0.44; invented 0.50; held-out lexemes 0.64), while Qwen3-4B and OLMo-7B meet it directionally. No model meets any other branch. The decision below therefore stands as **heuristics-extension**, met in full for one model and in direction for two.
Original text: No pre-registered branch criterion is met in full. Applying the rules literally, the paper branch is the one whose *direction* every measurement supports and whose thresholds fall short only in magnitude: **heuristics-extension**, stated with the shortfalls: (i) the model's own judgments follow its lexical pairwise table rather than dimension equality even under full-residual patching (73% vs 40% lawful flips); (ii) a causally effective unit representation exists at the first unit token (layers 4–8), is high-rank (effect still growing at rank 128), generalises to held-out lexemes, is not dormant, is necessary (noising 49%), dose-monotone, and does not contaminate quantity 2; (iii) it is *not* a pure dimension variable: it is bound with the numeral (value readout R² 0.96 → 0.55 after the swap), it has no effect for invented units (whose dimension lives in the definition sentence), and its effects on the disagree set follow the lexical table. Together with G1 (categorical, no lattice geometry) the honest headline is: **models carry a categorical, lexeme-general dimension code (G1) that is causally read only as part of a bound number–unit representation whose downstream use matches a pairwise-compatibility heuristic, not a lattice equality test.** Confidence: medium-high for Qwen3-4B (4 seeds, full control battery), medium for OLMo-7B (one seed, no controls), medium for Gemma-2-9B (bias-corrected readout, one seed, no controls; the strongest separation of the three).

## Rejected branch steelman (algebra / categorical-causal)
One can argue: the equality model is penalised by the model's *behavioural* errors — IIA under alg labels is bounded by the ceiling 0.65–0.67, and the subspace reaches 92% of that ceiling; on held-out lexemes it generalises, and probe-derived categorical directions carry it. So "the dimension category is causally used, the model just applies it imperfectly". I reject this as the headline because (a) where the two models make different predictions the effects follow the lexical table, (b) the equality-trained subspace is *better* described by the lexical labels than by equality, (c) invented units — the only case that separates "dimension from context" from "lexeme" — show zero effect at the unit token, and (d) the swap is not value-free. The steelman survives as a legitimate secondary framing: "imperfect categorical dimension use", and the paper reports the ceiling-normalised numbers so readers can weigh it.

## What would reverse this
IIA ≥ 0.65 on invented units at *any* site (e.g. the definition sentence or the referent), or a low-rank (≤ 16) subspace with alg ≥ heur on the disagree subset, in two models.

## Deviations declared
Balanced base-relative IIA and the rank-calibrated M_heur threshold were fixed after an uninformative pipeline test and before PREDICTIONS_phase2; the controls script's layer mismatch was found and fixed before its numbers were used; Gemma's readout is bias-corrected post hoc because its raw argmax is constant (declared, reported separately).
